# Cozy Casual
> Category: Game Art Direction
> Gentle life-sim, farming, crafting, decorating, collection, friendship, and low-pressure mobile/desktop game UI.

## 1. Art Direction & Atmosphere

Cozy Casual should feel welcoming, tactile, and unhurried without becoming bland. It supports farming sims, cafe builders, creature care, decorating games, cozy puzzles, idle collections, and social life-sim loops.

The style is warm but not beige-only. Use seasonal color, craft materials, soft shadows, rounded but disciplined forms, and clear interaction states. The player should feel safe experimenting. Hidden punishments and stressful urgency do not belong unless the game intentionally mixes cozy with danger and signposts it clearly.

## 2. Color Palette & Semantic Roles

Core palette:

- Warm paper: `oklch(93% 0.035 85)` for panels.
- Meadow green: `oklch(72% 0.12 145)` for growth, harvest, positive state.
- Sky blue: `oklch(78% 0.10 225)` for calm information and water.
- Berry red: `oklch(66% 0.15 25)` for affection, not lethal danger by default.
- Sunflower: `oklch(84% 0.15 92)` for rewards, quest focus, warmth.
- Clay: `oklch(63% 0.08 55)` for craft, soil, structure.
- Lavender: `oklch(76% 0.09 310)` for dreams, magic, premium but gentle moments.
- Ink brown: `oklch(28% 0.035 70)` for readable text.

Gameplay tokens:

- Ready to harvest: green glow plus leaf icon.
- Needs care: amber dot or speech bubble, not alarming red.
- Relationship gain: heart/flower animation in berry or pink.
- Craft complete: sunflower pulse and soft chime.
- Locked recipe: muted clay with visible requirement.
- Rare collectible: lavender or sky-blue frame with star shape.
- Error: friendly explanation, not harsh alarm language.

Use danger red only when the game has real danger. In most cozy games, red means affection, fruit, or warmth.

## 3. Typography Rules

Use friendly rounded or humanist fonts with strong readability. Avoid overly childish type unless the audience and game call for it. Body text should be large enough for handheld play.

Rules:

- Button labels should be short and plain.
- Dialogue needs comfortable line height.
- Counters and timers should not feel threatening unless urgency is intended.
- Recipe requirements need clear item names and quantities.
- Use icons with text for important resources.

## 4. HUD Density & Layout

Cozy UI should reduce cognitive load.

- Top area: day, time, weather, currency, energy if used.
- Bottom area: tool belt, quick actions, inventory access.
- Side panel: quest or goal tracker with optional pinning.
- Build/decorate mode: grid, rotate, confirm/cancel, undo.
- Crafting mode: category tabs, recipe list, required materials, preview.
- Relationship mode: character list, gift preferences, event state.
- Collection mode: album, museum, recipe book, creature journal.

Use generous touch targets for mobile and controller-friendly focus states for console/TV.

## 5. Gameplay Module Styling

- Panels: soft paper, cloth, wood, ceramic, or enamel with subtle texture.
- Buttons: pill or tab shapes are allowed when consistent, but avoid oversized marketing cards.
- Inventory slots: rounded squares, stable size, item icon, stack count, rarity/quality tag.
- Recipe cards: output preview, input row, time, station, missing materials.
- Dialogue boxes: character portrait, name, short lines, history access.
- Calendars: seasonal color bands, icons, clear event duration.
- Shop rows: item, price, owned count, restock timer if relevant.

Keep depth shallow. Cozy interfaces should feel tactile, not like a pile of floating panels.

## 6. Motion, Game Feel & Feedback

Motion should feel soft and responsive.

- Harvest: pop, bounce under 6 percent scale, item arc.
- Craft complete: gentle pulse and sparkle, no flash flood.
- Relationship gain: small heart/flower drift, capped quantity.
- Drag/drop: item lift shadow and slot highlight.
- Decoration placement: ghost preview, grid snap, confirm puff.
- Daily summary: calm count-up with skip option.
- Error: small head shake or wiggle plus helpful text.

Reduced motion should use opacity and outline. Avoid endless idle bouncing on many objects at once.

## 7. VFX, Materials & Scene Direction

Materials:

- Paper, wood, ceramic, cloth, felt, woven baskets.
- Soft water, leaves, soil, flour, wool, clay.
- Seasonal overlays: spring petals, summer sun, autumn leaves, winter snow.
- Gentle magic as fireflies, small stars, or soft glows.

VFX should be sparse and meaningful. A farm full of sparkling icons becomes visual clutter. Use priority: urgent care, ready action, selected object, special event.

## 8. Audio Direction Tokens

- UI hover/select: soft tap, wood click, bell, or cloth rustle.
- Harvest: pluck or pop.
- Plant: soil pat.
- Craft complete: small chime.
- Relationship gain: warm two-note motif.
- Error: soft "not yet" sound, never scolding.
- Season/event: short musical identity.
- Idle collect: satisfying but low-frequency sound to avoid fatigue.

Audio should make routine pleasant without becoming sugary repetition.

## 9. Do's and Don'ts

Do:

- Make next actions obvious and low-pressure.
- Use color variety across seasons, resources, and characters.
- Let players undo, move, preview, and experiment.
- Make missing requirements helpful.
- Keep timers honest and gentle.
- Support one-thumb mobile and controller focus.

Do not:

- Hide severe penalties behind cute UI.
- Turn every daily goal into obligation.
- Use beige and cream for everything until hierarchy disappears.
- Make tiny resource icons without labels.
- Ask for ratings, notifications, or purchases before the player has value.
- Overanimate farms, towns, or menus until they feel noisy.

## 10. Agent Prompt Guide

When using Cozy Casual, include the player's daily goal, care loop, crafting or collection state, relationship context, and available undo/preview affordances. Use warm tactile materials, friendly but clear labels, flexible goals, and feedback that celebrates progress without pressuring attendance.
