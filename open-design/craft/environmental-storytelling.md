# Environmental Storytelling

Use when building world story through space, props, lighting, layout, sound, and discovery.

## Principles

- Show what happened, who wanted what, what changed, and what the player can infer.
- Props should imply use, ownership, scale, culture, conflict, or time.
- Lighting and composition guide attention before UI labels do.
- Repeated motifs build world rules; one-off decoration becomes noise.
- Optional discovery should reward curiosity without hiding critical comprehension.

## Checklist

- Each location has a before/after or cause/effect story.
- Props are grouped by character, faction, event, or system logic.
- The player can distinguish critical path, optional lore, hazard, and reward.
- Audio or ambient motion reinforces the scene's emotional beat.
- World details do not contradict mechanics or faction motives.

## Production Notes

- Separate must-read story clues from optional flavor so level design can protect progression readability.
- Budget bespoke props for narrative beats that change player decisions; reuse modular dressing for background culture and history.
- Track ownership, faction, time period, and damage state in asset briefs so outsourced environment art stays coherent.
- Validate environmental storytelling in playtests by asking what players inferred before explaining the lore.
