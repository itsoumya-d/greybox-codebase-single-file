# Boss Design Frameworks

Use when designing bosses, elite encounters, multi-phase fights, raid mechanics, set-piece duels, and mechanic exams. A boss should test what the game has taught while revealing a memorable personality, rule, or threat.

## What To Produce

- Boss fantasy and combat thesis.
- Phase plan with mechanics introduced, combined, and escalated.
- Attack table with tells, ranges, timings, punish windows, and counters.
- Arena plan with hazards, recovery zones, camera needs, and phase changes.
- Difficulty levers for damage, health, aggression, adds, resources, and timing.
- Accessibility and readability notes.
- Rewards and narrative consequences.

## Boss Thesis

Every boss needs a sentence that describes the fight's job.

Examples:

- "A shield captain who teaches patience by punishing frontal attacks and rewarding flanks."
- "A swamp hag who turns healing into a positioning puzzle."
- "A racing rival who tests drift control under changing weather."
- "A raid machine that requires role swaps between shield, interrupt, and objective control."

Without a thesis, bosses become attack collections. The player should feel a coherent intelligence, creature, machine, or system behind the fight.

## Phase Structure

Phases should change decisions, not only health bars.

| Phase | Purpose | Common Tools |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Teach the boss language | Clear tells, limited move set, safe punish windows |
| Phase 2 | Combine mechanics | Faster chains, arena hazards, adds, resource pressure |
| Phase 3 | Stress mastery | Fewer safe windows, mixed tells, phase-specific risk |
| Enrage | Optional climax | Timer, high pressure, spectacle, narrow but fair finish |

Use 2 phases for most campaign bosses, 3 for major bosses, and more only when the fight supports a long learning arc or raid structure.

## Attack Table

Design attacks as readable contracts.

| Attack | Tell | Range | Counter | Punish Window | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cleave | Weapon shoulder raise | Front cone | Dodge through or flank | 1.2s recovery | Teaches angle |
| Slam | Ground glow and audio swell | Circle AoE | Exit zone, jump, block | 1.6s recovery | Camera shake reduced option |
| Summon | Boss kneels at altar | Arena-wide | Interrupt or clear adds | None if ignored | Creates priority shift |
| Grab | Open hand, forward lean | Short lunge | Backstep, parry | 0.8s recovery | High damage, long tell |

Do not use identical tells for attacks with different counters unless deception is the explicit advanced mechanic and the player has a fair read.

## Tells And Readability

Tells should be layered:

- Pose: silhouette change before motion.
- Audio: unique cue for major danger.
- VFX: ground marker, trail, charge glow, weak-point flash.
- Timing: consistent startup window for learning.
- Camera: framing that shows the threat without stealing control.
- UI: boss bar states, phase warnings, accessibility cues.

Readable does not mean easy. It means the player can learn the contract.

## Punish And Recovery Windows

Bosses need windows where player skill turns into progress.

- Short punish windows reward precision and aggressive builds.
- Long punish windows reward survival and learning.
- Positional windows reward route planning.
- Resource windows reward conservation.
- Objective windows reward team coordination.

If the boss attacks constantly with no recovery, the fight becomes waiting for mercy. If punish windows are too long, the boss loses authority.

## Arena Design

The arena is part of the boss move set.

- Shape controls movement: circle for radial reads, lanes for charges, pillars for line-of-sight, platforms for traversal.
- Safe zones should be readable but not permanent.
- Hazards should support the phase thesis.
- Edges need clear rules: death drop, bounce, barrier, or recovery.
- Camera occlusion must be tested from common player positions.
- Multiplayer arenas need role spacing and revive safety.

Phase changes can transform the arena through flooding, collapsing cover, opening vertical routes, changing lighting, adding hazards, or shifting objective zones.

## Adds And Secondary Objectives

Adds are useful only if they create a meaningful priority decision.

Good uses:

- Force target switching.
- Create resource or space pressure.
- Give the player a way to regain health, ammo, or special charge.
- Teach a mechanic used later by the boss.
- Support co-op role distribution.

Weak uses:

- Spawn enemies only to extend runtime.
- Hide the boss in visual noise.
- Punish single-player builds that cannot handle crowd control.
- Create unavoidable off-screen damage.

## Difficulty Levers

Use layered levers so difficulty modes preserve identity:

- Damage taken and dealt.
- Boss health or phase thresholds.
- Tell duration.
- Recovery window length.
- Add count and spawn cadence.
- Resource drops.
- Arena hazard frequency.
- Assist indicators.
- Checkpoints between phases.

Avoid removing mechanics on easier modes when possible. Widen windows and reduce punishment first, so players still learn the boss.

## Rewards

A boss reward should answer the fight's emotional promise.

- Mechanical: new ability, weapon, class option, traversal verb.
- Narrative: revealed truth, faction shift, companion change.
- Spatial: new region, shortcut, base upgrade.
- Cosmetic: trophy, banner, armor skin, title.
- Social: raid clear badge, leaderboard, clan progress.

Reward timing matters. Let the player breathe before opening management screens.

## Anti-Patterns

- Phase changes that only refill health.
- One-shot attacks with weak tells.
- Bosses immune to most player builds without warning.
- Adds that create visual noise instead of decisions.
- Arenas that fight the camera more than the player.
- Random attack selection that creates impossible overlaps.
- Difficulty modes that make the fight tedious through health inflation.

## Agent Checklist

- What is the boss testing that the game has already taught?
- Does each phase introduce, combine, or escalate a clear idea?
- Are attack tells distinct, readable, and tied to counters?
- Are punish windows defined for different player styles?
- Does the arena support the fight thesis?
- Are adds or objectives meaningful rather than filler?
- Does the reward match the emotional and mechanical importance of the boss?
