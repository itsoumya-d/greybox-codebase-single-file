# Genre Benchmarking

Genre benchmarks compare design problems and player expectations, not surface imitation.

## What To Produce

- A benchmark table with genre leader, relevant lesson, risk if copied blindly, and adaptation for this game.
- A pacing comparison for onboarding, session length, difficulty, progression, and replayability.
- A differentiation statement that protects the game's own fantasy.

## Design Rules

- Benchmark mechanics at the same scale as the creator can ship.
- Compare player jobs: aim, dodge, plan, collect, socialize, explore, optimize, survive, or express identity.
- Identify required genre promises and optional genre conventions.
- Call out where the game should intentionally break convention.
- Avoid using benchmarks as art-style shortcuts without gameplay reasoning.

## Production Gates

- Estimate whether each benchmarked system is solo, indie, AA, or AAA feasible.
- Name the hidden cost: animation count, content volume, online operations, balance testing, or UI complexity.
- Include one smaller alternative for any benchmark that implies a large team.

## Agent Checklist

- Compare at least three genre expectations.
- Include production feasibility.
- Include player motivation and frustration risks.
- Include a clear differentiation angle.
